Night trains return to Central Europe

“We are bringing the night train back,” the transport minister said, as sleeper services linking Vienna, Rome and Amsterdam were confirmed for December.

By Marta Nowak · 14 September 2026

SharePostEmail

After a decade of cuts, overnight trains are coming back to the heart of Europe. From 13 December, sleepers will run every night between Vienna and Rome, and three times a week between Vienna and Amsterdam, the rail operators announced on Monday.

The new timetable starts in December. Sleepers leave Vienna at 19:40 and reach Rome at 09:55, with a stop in Florence. Couchettes cost from 69 euros, a private compartment with a shower from 199 euros, and bikes travel for 15 euros.

Why now

Demand for rail travel has grown every year since 2022, and short-haul flights are getting more expensive, the operators said. A night train replaces both a flight and a hotel night, which makes the fares easier to compare than they look.

Speaking at Vienna’s central station, the transport minister told reporters: “We are bringing the night train back, and this time it is here to stay.” The government will cover part of the track fees for the first three years.

Environmental groups welcomed the plan but asked for more routes to the north and east, where rail links are still slow. The operators said Berlin and Warsaw are being studied for 2028.

Tips for a first night on the train

  • Book a lower berth if you prefer to sit up in the evening.
  • Bring a small bag for the night: big suitcases go under the seats.
  • Breakfast is included in sleeper compartments, not in couchettes.

Rail fans have waited a long time for this. “The train is slower than a plane, but the journey becomes part of the holiday,” said Ingrid Hofer, who has travelled by night train across Europe for thirty years and plans to take the first Vienna to Rome service in December.
